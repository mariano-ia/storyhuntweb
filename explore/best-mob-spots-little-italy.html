Best Mob Spots in Little Italy

Little Italy, once the heart of Italian-American culture, remains steeped in mob lore. While most tourists flock to the bustling restaurants along Mulberry Street, they often miss the hidden gems that tell the real story of organized crime in this neighborhood. Locations like the former Copacabana nightclub at 10 E 60th St, where mobsters mingled with celebrities, and the old St. Patrick's Cathedral at 263 Mulberry St, which served as a backdrop for many clandestine meetings, offer a glimpse into the shadowy world of the Mafia. Understanding this history provides depth to the Little Italy experience, revealing the complex relationship between the community and organized crime that shaped this area in the 20th century.

To dive deeper into Little Italy's mobster past, start at the infamous John Gotti's favorite eatery, the Ravenite Social Club, located at 247 Mulberry St. Now a souvenir shop, this was once a hotspot for the Gambino family. Next, head to 332 E 2nd St, the former residence of mob boss Carlo Gambino, where you can still feel the echoes of decisions made in smoke-filled rooms. Don't miss the former site of the Five Points neighborhood, where the notorious Five Points Gang ruled; it's near the intersection of Worth and Baxter Streets. For a chilling reminder of the Mafia's darker side, visit the site of the 1931 murder of mobster Joe Masseria at 12th St & 1st Ave. Finally, take a stroll down Mott Street and look for the old storefronts, many of which have been in the same family for generations, holding stories that are as rich as the pasta served in the trattorias. Best times to visit are early mornings or late afternoons when the streets are quieter, allowing you to absorb the history without the crowds.
