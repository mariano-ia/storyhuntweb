Best Hidden Places in SoHo

SoHo, known for its cast-iron architecture and vibrant art scene, often attracts throngs of visitors drawn to its upscale shops and trendy eateries. However, many miss the hidden gems that lie just off the beaten path. These spots embody the neighborhood's rich history and unique character. For instance, at 40 Mercer Street, you’ll find the historic site of the first SoHo art gallery, which helped launch the neighborhood as a creative hub in the 1970s. Just a block away, at the corner of Spring and Greene Streets, lies a discreet courtyard where artists once gathered to discuss their work, now overshadowed by the surrounding commercialism. Visiting these sites provides a glimpse into SoHo's evolution, revealing stories of the artists and entrepreneurs who shaped its identity.

To uncover the hidden treasures of SoHo, consider these recommendations: Start at 33 Wooster Street, home to the original Dean & DeLuca, where the gourmet grocery store transformed food culture in the area. Next, head over to 2 Greene Street, where you can spot the remnants of the historic SoHo cast-iron facades, perfect for photography lovers. Don’t miss the hidden garden at 207 Mott Street, accessible through an unmarked entrance; it's a serene escape from the bustling streets. Also, explore 36 Howard Street, where you’ll find the former home of the famous artist Keith Haring, now adorned with a mural that pays tribute to his legacy. Finally, visit the tiny alley known as MacDougal Alley, stretching between Mercer and Greene Streets, which showcases a blend of historic architecture and modern street art. Aim to visit these places early in the morning or during weekdays to avoid crowds and fully appreciate their charm.
